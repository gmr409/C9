What It Is

The world’s longest-running sports car returns for its ninth generation in 2030. The C9 Chevrolet Corvette will be the second iteration of the mid-engine Corvette and the product of many valuable lessons learned from the C8. The biggest changes will be visual. The C8 Corvette, for all its merits, is neither a classically pretty Corvette nor a well-proportioned mid-engine sports car. Furthermore, owners weren’t in love with the cabin’s closed-off cockpit feel, an issue partially rectified by the Corvette’s 2026 face-lift.

Our sources say the new C9 Corvette will be heavily influenced by the GM Design Center–penned 2025 Corvette CX concept, with softer, more cohesive lines that recall the Coke-bottle hips of earlier front-engine Corvettes without sacrificing the performance progress made by the transition to a mid-engine layout.

Why It Matters

For the past 30 years or so, the Corvette line has served as a high-performance proving ground for the rest of General Motors. Technologies such as Magnetic Ride Control and Performance Traction Management have been developed for and perfected by various Corvette generations. The C8 in particular has been a tour de force from an engineering perspective, led by the development of both naturally aspirated and turbocharged versions of the world’s largest flat-plane-crank V-8 in the Z06 and ZR1, respectively. Electrification was also introduced to the lineup for the first time in both the E-Ray and ZR1X. Although we don’t expect the C9 to go full electric at launch, we absolutely do expect to continue seeing it on the bleeding edge of the performance envelope during its life cycle.

Platform and Powertrain

There’s a lot still taking shape when it comes to what powers the C9 Corvette, so the only sure thing is that the car will ride on an updated (and hopefully lighter) version of the C8’s architecture. Despite two of three recent Corvette concepts being envisioned as four-motor electric-hypercars, we expect the C9 to stick with a traditional pushrod V-8 at the outset, though we could see a fully electric-powered version at some point in the early 2030s.

The new 2027 Corvette Grand Sport (Classic kicks) marked the debut of Chevy’s new LT6 6.7-liter V-8, which makes 535 hp and 520 lb-ft of torque and is paired with an eight-speed dual-clutch automatic. We strongly suspect that will be the powertrain we’ll see in the base Stingray when the C9 arrives in 2029 as a 2030 model. We also expect the usual slate of higher-performance variants, with more powerful hybrids (perhaps one with dual front motors versus the single now found in the ZR1X and Grand Sport X) added to the traditional mix of Z51, Grand Sport, Z06, and ZR1 models.
